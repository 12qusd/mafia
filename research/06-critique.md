# Adversarial Critique — Risks, Gaps, Pushback

An experienced-indie-multiplayer-dev review of the whole dossier. Read this before trusting
any individual recommendation above.

## Top risks

### Risk

Population cold-start is the existential risk and the dossier documents it without solving it. A 15-player lobby needs roughly 10x the concurrent pool of Among Us's 4-10 player rooms; the dossier's own comps show every matchmade deduction game under ~300 CCU is functionally dead (Throne of Lies 1.4k peak -> 1 CCU, Project Winter 2.9k -> 11). A text-only clone of a 2011 SC2 Arcade map launches with zero acquisition channel: no streamer appeal, no marketing budget, no existing funnel. The most likely failure mode is launching to empty lobbies, collecting 'can't find a game' Steam reviews that are permanent, and spiraling within 4-8 weeks.

**Mitigation:** Design out dependence on stranger matchmaking: make bring-your-own-group private lobbies the first-class mode (the dossier's own werewolf.chat/Mafia.gg lesson — BYO-group tools are death-spiral-immune); support 7-15 players with auto-scaling role lists so a 9-person lobby starts instead of waiting; recruit the SC2Mafia diaspora, UltiMafia, and Discord mafia communities as founding hosts BEFORE launch; ship scheduled-event tooling (community game nights) instead of pretending a quickmatch queue will fill; launch the free browser client months before Steam to build the pool, and only pull the Steam launch trigger when there's a measurable nightly population.

### Risk

Solo-dev playtest impossibility is the hidden schedule killer. Every balance/UX iteration of a 15-player, 30-60 minute game requires 10-15 simultaneous humans; that throttles iteration to maybe two real sessions a week, and the genre's fun lives entirely in emergent multi-player dynamics no unit test reaches. The dossier never addresses how one person tests this game at all.

**Mitigation:** Build the deterministic headless simulator and scripted bot clients on day one — the dossier's own (seed, intents) -> deterministic-resolution design enables this; add property-based and golden-case tests for the night-resolution pipeline; run 1-human-plus-14-bots smoke tests for every flow; use Steam Playtest (free, separate app, missing from the dossier's shipping steps) plus a standing Discord playtest group with scheduled sessions; accept that bots verify mechanics, not the social game, and budget calendar time for human playtests accordingly.

### Risk

Night-resolution engine correctness will be litigated by the community, and silent bugs are trust-fatal: in a deduction game, a wrong Sheriff result is indistinguishable from server cheating to the player. The published resolution order includes a 'repeat the Witch->BusDriver->Roleblocker cycle a random number of times' paradox solver, an acknowledged Jailor witch-immunity bug, and dozens of conversion/redirection edge cases documented only as wiki prose and forum rulings — a reimplementation from that material will diverge in ways veterans will catch in week one.

**Mitigation:** Write your own formal spec rather than transliterating the wiki; replace 'random number of iterations' with deterministic fixed-point iteration plus a documented tie-break; build an exhaustive interaction test suite seeded from the wiki edge cases (witch-on-witch, escort-blocks-escort-blocking-witch, MM-at-jailed-target); publish the resolution spec so the community debugs alongside you; expose per-match replays/audits from (seed, intents) so disputes resolve with evidence; decide explicitly, per mechanic, whether you want bug-for-bug fidelity or a clean fix, and document it.

### Risk

Product-market contradiction the dossier states but never reconciles: its comps section proves the genre acquires players via streamers, built-in voice, and short clippable rounds — and the game being cloned is the exact opposite (text-only, 30-60 minute matches, dense jargon, dead players idling in a graveyard). Drifting into that gap means building a product with neither the niche depth veterans demand nor the casual funnel strangers need.

**Mitigation:** Force the positioning decision now: commit to being a niche community product for the text-mafia audience (MafiaScum/EpicMafia/SC2Mafia lineage), size expectations to a sustainable 100-500 CCU at near-zero burn, and lean into what that audience values — host tools, setup sharing, spectating, replays, private lobbies. Still ship a spectator/streamer mode (hide own-role/whispers from capture) and make trial/execution moments visually clippable, because streamer waves are free re-seeding even for niche titles (Feign's Turkish-streamer cycles).

### Risk

Scope blowout. Even the 15-role MVP plus the setup editor, per-role option toggles, four day types, trials, whispers, wills, reconnection, moderation tooling, bot/test harness, and the Steam pipeline is realistically 12-24 months solo; the full 60-role surface with Cult conversion bookkeeping, Triad, and the option matrix is multi-year. First Steam release + first multiplayer game + first game ever is a compounding-unknowns stack.

**Mitigation:** Cut below the dossier's MVP: one day type (Majority + Trial), 2-3 fixed curated setups instead of the custom editor, 9-12 players; ship the setup editor and option toggles as the post-launch retention beat (they're also the features that multiply the QA matrix); hold Triad/Cult/Bus Driver/Witch until the game is live and stable; timebox a playable 9-player vertical slice (lobby -> roles -> day/vote/trial -> night -> win check) in 6-8 weeks as a go/no-go checkpoint.

### Risk

The real legal threat is takedown asymmetry, not a lawsuit. The dossier's 'approximately zero' Blizzard-risk conclusion is sound, but a cheap Steam complaint — from BlankMediaGames on look-and-feel, 2K on the 'Mafia' video-game trademark, or even a troll — can delist a live multiplayer game for weeks during dispute resolution, which is fatal to a small population even when you'd ultimately win.

**Mitigation:** Pick an original title with distance from both 'SC2' and standalone 'Mafia'; build a theme visually distinct from both StarCraft and Town of Salem's Salem trade dress; rename the distinctive-tier roles (Triad set, Beguiler, Auditor) as the dossier says; document the clean-room process (sources used, no extracted assets) contemporaneously; have a counter-notice template and a games-IP lawyer contact ready before launch, not after the takedown lands.

### Risk

Moderation and child-safety load on one person. Accusation-as-core-loop chat plus minors plus (likely) free accounts creates real obligations — DSA notice-and-action, and the UK Online Safety Act duties the dossier omits entirely — and a solo dev cannot staff a 72-hour human review queue at any traction level. EpicMafia died of governance, not technology; that lesson cuts against both no-moderation and hastily-deputized volunteer moderation.

**Mitigation:** Build the data model day one (reports, sanctions, append-only match_events — the dossier is right here) but phase the ceremony with scale; automate first: server-side evasion-aware filtering, rate limits, heuristic flags; when volunteers become necessary, give them scoped, fully-logged tools and a removal process (the EpicMafia failure was unaccountable mods); add an age gate and consider restricted chat defaults for minors; read the OSA's small-service duties before launch since UK players are inevitable.

### Risk

Platform fragility in the recommended stack: steamworks.js is effectively single-maintainer with 67 open issues; the Steam overlay is broken on macOS and flaky on Windows for Electron; Steam Deck runs the Windows Electron build under Proton where a text-input-heavy game is borderline; and the dossier cites Valve C++ API features (FilterText, ShowFloatingGamepadTextInput, ICheatReportingService) without verifying the JS binding actually exposes them.

**Mitigation:** Wrap all Steamworks access behind a thin adapter so swapping to a fork or raw steamworks-rs is a contained change; keep Steam surface area minimal (auth tickets, achievements, rich presence — the dossier's own advice); spike-test the specific binding calls you depend on in week one, not at integration time; route invites through Steam-client launch args from the start instead of fighting the overlay; target Deck 'Playable' at most and don't spend MVP time on controller UI.

### Risk

Off-platform collusion and dead-chat omniscience break stranger matches by design: friends in Discord voice sharing roles is technically unsolvable, and SC2Mafia's dead players see ALL hidden information — one dead friend in a voice call ruins the game for the living. Ranked integrity (win-trading, gamethrowing, multi-accounting, the Jester/thrower ambiguity) has no concrete design anywhere in the dossier.

**Mitigation:** Make dead-player information visibility a per-mode setting: full omniscience in private lobbies (it's fun with friends), restricted-until-game-end in matchmade games; accept collusion in private lobbies as out of scope; defer ranked entirely (see pushback) but log everything now so behavioral detection (vote-pattern anomalies, premature-knowledge tells) is possible later; treat 'reported for gamethrowing' as the dominant report category it will be and design the triage UI for it.

### Risk

Browser+Steam dual-SKU complexity arrives earlier than the dossier admits: two distribution channels mean identity unification (SteamID vs email/web accounts), version skew between always-fresh browser clients and Steam-updated desktop clients hitting one server fleet, double the cheat/abuse surface (devtools-open browser client makes scripted clients trivial), and running two release pipelines as one person.

**Mitigation:** One account system from day one with Steam as a linked auth provider rather than a parallel identity; server-enforced protocol version with a force-refresh/update message; sequence rather than parallelize — browser-first launch, Steam SKU only after traction — so you never operate both channels before product-market fit; assume hostile scripted clients from the start (the server-authoritative design already covers correctness; add rate limiting and account-quality gates for abuse).

## Missing considerations

- Phase-timer client sync and deadline races: server-side timers are specified, but not countdown drift correction on clients, what happens to a vote/night-action packet that arrives 200ms after phase cutoff (need an authoritative grace-window policy), 'Trial Pauses Day' timer arithmetic, or re-sync after tab sleep/background throttling in the browser build.
- AFK/leaver design depth: does leaving reveal your role (information leak either way — SC2Mafia 'suicides' the leaver but their queued kill still lands)? Role-dodge quitting right after assignment, AFK detection escalation (default action -> seat kill -> bot replacement?), lobby ready-checks, and a leaver-penalty system. All of this hits in the first week of human playtests and none is specified.
- Lobby host powers and migration: host disconnect during setup/naming phase, the -repick vote, kick/ban from lobby, and host griefing via degenerate setups in public lobbies (the SC2Mafia 'save built offline because lobbies get impatient' etiquette is itself a UX failure to fix, e.g. setup browser/vetted presets, not replicate).
- Variable player-count support: every SC2Mafia setup assumes 15 players; matchmade reality is 7-12. Auto-scaling role lists with balance rules per count is a significant missing design system, and the single biggest lever against the cold-start problem.
- Bots as two separate problems: scripted bots for testing (achievable, essential) versus bots for lobby backfill (a social deduction bot that chats convincingly is out of solo scope; LLM bots bring cost, latency, and moderation risk). The dossier should not let backfill bots be assumed as a cold-start solution.
- Steam Playtest and Next Fest are absent from the shipping steps — Steam Playtest is the canonical free mechanism for pre-launch multiplayer testing at scale, and a Next Fest demo is the main wishlist-building event for an unknown multiplayer title.
- New-player onboarding: this is a 60-role jargon-dense genre (N1, WIFOM, claims) and the dossier has zero on tutorialization — in-game role cards, contextual ability help, a bots-only tutorial match, glossary, simplified default setups for new accounts. Onboarding is the genre's biggest funnel filter.
- Art and asset budget: nothing in the profile suggests art skills, and the legal analysis demands an original visual identity. Capsule art, role icons, execution animations, store assets, and any cosmetics pipeline must be contracted — unbudgeted money and schedule, and 'original look and feel' is precisely the legal mitigation, so it can't be skipped.
- The monetization decision for THIS game is never actually made: comps conclude 'free or under $5, never gate matches,' but the dossier ends without a recommendation. F2P on Steam invites the bot/ban-evasion problems the dossier itself documents (Among Us, ToS1); paid suppresses the population it can't afford to lose. This is a core open decision, not a detail.
- Deploy/ops with live games: you cannot restart the room server mid-match. Needs graceful drain (stop allocating new rooms, wait out running games), per-process room sharding or blue-green from day one, and a stated crash-recovery policy — the dossier calls event-log recovery a 'nice-to-have,' which stops being true the day real users exist.
- Localization and language-split lobbies: chat-based deduction is unplayable cross-language, so the lobby list/queue must segment by language; the Feign lesson implies deliberately picking one or two launch languages and owning those communities. String externalization is near-free in the web stack if done from day one, expensive retrofitted.
- Accessibility specifics: faction identity is literally red/green (colorblind-hostile — needs shapes/labels), text scaling, keyboard-only play, reduced-motion for execution sequences, and screen-reader-friendly DOM — the last being a genuine web-stack advantage no engine competitor can match, worth exploiting deliberately.
- Telemetry and population health instrumentation: lobby wait time, match completion rate, first-session retention, geographic/timezone concentration — the comps explicitly say 'alive only at 7pm EST' is the death-spiral early-warning, but the dossier specs no analytics to detect it.
- Spectator mode and a replay viewer as concrete scoped features: the event log enables them, but the UI is real work; also a streamer-safety mode (hide own role/whispers from capture until reveal) given streamability is the genre's only free acquisition channel.
- Chat infrastructure mundanities: per-channel rate limiting, paste-flood and mention spam control, link/markup sanitization (the Godot section worries about BBCode injection but the web stack has its own XSS-in-chat surface), whisper metadata rules, persistent client-side mutes.
- Verification that steamworks.js exposes the specific APIs the plan leans on (FilterText, ShowFloatingGamepadTextInput, overlay-invite flows) — the dossier cites Valve's C++ documentation throughout but never checks binding coverage; ICheatReportingService game bans are a server-side Web API call and fine, but client-side surface needs a spike.
- Marketing beyond shipping mechanics: the Steam section covers how to ship, not how anyone finds out — wishlist campaign, dev-logging to the SC2Mafia/ToS diaspora (Reddit r/TownofSalem2, sc2mafia forum remnants, UltiMafia Discord), browser-portal distribution (itch.io, CrazyGames) for the web client, and the explicit 'why switch from free UltiMafia/ToS2' differentiation argument, which the dossier never makes.
- Bug-for-bug fidelity vs clean redesign as an explicit, documented per-mechanic decision (Jailor witch-immunity bug, random-iteration paradox solver, Triad-beats-Mafia tie rules): veterans will demand fidelity, newcomers won't care, and drifting silently between the two produces the worst of both.

## Pushback on the dossier's own recommendations

- Steam-first sequencing is wrong-ordered relative to the dossier's own best insight. If the browser version is the population-seeding strategy, it should LAUNCH first — Steam 6-12 months later once nightly lobbies demonstrably fill. The Steam release is a one-shot visibility event (new-release feed, first reviews); burning it on empty lobbies converts the cold-start problem into permanent 'dead game' reviews. The 4-6 week Steam pipeline and $100 fee should wait for traction, not anchor the plan.
- macOS effort is heavily overweighted: $99/yr Apple Developer, notarization yak-shaving, entitlements gotchas — for a platform that is single-digit-percent of this audience, where the Steam overlay is broken anyway, and which the browser build covers completely. Ship Windows+web at launch; add the Mac depot post-traction if Mac players actually materialize.
- Colyseus StateView is oversold as the security lever. Tagging fields to hide is a blacklist model; for hidden-information secrecy you want whitelist-by-default — explicit per-client event sends where nothing reaches a socket unless deliberately addressed to it. That's trivially built on plain ws + zod with the dev's existing skills, and the 'you'd rebuild 60% of Colyseus' claim is inflated: the hard 60% (phase state machine, night resolution, per-client filtering discipline) is game-specific and yours to write regardless. Colyseus is a fine convenience choice; it is not what makes the game cheat-proof, and boardgame.io's own secrets-leaked-via-side-paths issue (#399) shows why send-discipline beats field-tagging.
- Glicko-2/ranked at launch is premature and mildly harmful: a ranked queue splits an already-thin population, and the dossier has no answer for win-trading, multi-accounting, or gamethrowing detection that ranked immediately invites. Launch unranked with visible stats; add ratings when CCU can support split queues. Keep the rating_before/rating_after schema columns — recomputable later from the event log anyway.
- The 15-player, 15-slot frame should not survive contact with launch reality: filling 15 strangers is brutal at realistic launch CCU. Make 9-12 the designed default with auto-scaling setups and let 15 be the community-event format. Same pushback applies to the option matrix — 4 day types x 3 night types x dozens of per-role toggles exists because SC2 Arcade hosts had captive lobbies; quickmatch strangers need 2-3 curated setups, and every toggle multiplies a QA matrix one person cannot cover. Ship the configurator late as a retention feature, not early as a foundation.
- The packet-size/timing side-channel padding advice is over-engineering for MVP: traffic-shape analysis to infer faction is a threat worth a backlog note, not launch work, for a sub-1k-CCU text game. Need-to-know serialization is the 99% defense; do that and move on.
- The DSA-shaped moderation prescription is compliance-overweight pre-traction: build the data model (reports, sanctions, evidence-linked event log) from day one — that part is correct and cheap — but a 72h human-review SLA and formal Article 16 notice flows for a game with 50 CCU is ceremony; a working server-side mute, a filter, and a Discord channel is the honest launch bar, with the formal machinery phased in as user counts justify it. Meanwhile the dossier omits the UK Online Safety Act, which in 2026 is arguably the more aggressive regime for a small chat platform.
- The comps section's scaling lessons (Unity Multiplay, elastic capacity 'ready before the viral moment') are mostly inapplicable and risk infra-anxiety spending: a text-mafia niche title will not 10,000x overnight, and if lightning somehow strikes, a room-based architecture shards horizontally fast enough. One box plus a tested drain-and-shard plan is genuinely sufficient; resist pre-buying for a spike that will not come.
- Hosting cost analysis prices the VPS but not the human: the binding constraint on a solo live-service operator is attention — on-call, moderation, community management, content cadence — which argues for the most boring possible stack (fewer moving parts beats framework features) and for designing community self-service (host tools, volunteer-runnable events) as load-shedding, not just as features.
- The dossier's two strongest sections argue against its implicit ambition and nobody calls it: the comps prove this genre rewards either viral casual products (voice, short rounds, streamable) or zero-burn community platforms (BYO-group, hostable, open-ish) — and punishes everything in between. A faithful SC2Mafia clone is structurally the second thing. The plan should explicitly embrace hobby-business economics (sub-$5 or free, ~100-500 CCU ceiling, near-zero burn, community-as-infrastructure) or explicitly diverge from the source design toward voice/short-rounds — the dossier currently lets him believe he can split the difference.
- 'Approximately zero' legal risk conflates probability with severity: the realistic vector is a cheap takedown complaint (BMG look-and-feel, 2K's 'Mafia' mark) delisting a population-dependent live game for weeks during dispute — low probability, but the mitigation (naming distance, theme distance, documented clean-room, counter-notice readiness) costs almost nothing and should be framed as mandatory, not optional polish.
- Trusting the dossier's Steamworks feature analysis without a binding spike is a trap: it consistently cites Valve's C++ API docs (lobbies, FilterText, floating keyboard, rich presence connect keys) while the actual dependency is steamworks.js's coverage of those calls in Electron. Week-one task: a throwaway Electron app exercising every Steam call the plan needs, on Windows and on a Deck, before any architecture hardens around them.
